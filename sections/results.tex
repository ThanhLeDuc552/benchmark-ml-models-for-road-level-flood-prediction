\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Flood Labels}
Of the 2,059 segments, 876 (42.5\%) are impassable in the simulation ($y=1$), 1,044 (50.7\%) are flooded but passable, and 139 (6.8\%) are passable. Only 6.8\% of the network stays below 10~cm, so the event affected almost the whole district. The impassable segments cluster along the low-lying bank of the Han River and around natural depressions, including the Dien Bien Phu underpass.

\subsection{Classification Benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:models} compares the six models, and Fig.~\ref{fig:curves} shows their ROC and precision--recall curves under spatial cross-validation. Random forest achieved the highest AUC-PR ($0.840 \pm 0.077$), followed by the MLP ($0.833 \pm 0.067$), XGBoost ($0.831 \pm 0.081$), and GraphSAGE ($0.823 \pm 0.083$); logistic regression was lowest ($0.784 \pm 0.111$). These differences are small compared with the variation between folds. The Friedman test found no significant difference among the six models ($\chi^2 = 7.86$, $p = 0.164$), and pairwise Wilcoxon tests against random forest gave $p = 0.0625$ for LR, the smallest value possible with five folds, and $p \ge 0.31$ for the other models. GraphSAGE reached the highest recall on impassable segments ($0.852 \pm 0.043$) and F1 ($0.791 \pm 0.057$), and random forest the lowest Brier score ($0.127 \pm 0.012$).

\subsection{Optimism of Random Cross-Validation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:gap} compares AUC-ROC under random and spatial cross-validation. Every model scored higher under random cross-validation, with optimism gaps $\Delta_m$ from $+0.034$ (LR) to $+0.094$ (random forest). The tree ensembles were inflated most (random forest $+0.094$, XGBoost $+0.085$), followed by GraphSAGE ($+0.077$), the MLP ($+0.054$), and the SVM ($+0.047$). Under random cross-validation, random forest would appear near-perfect (AUC-ROC 0.992); under spatial cross-validation, all models except LR lie between 0.889 and 0.897.

\subsection{Feature-Group Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports AUC-PR when each feature group is removed. Terrain features matter most: removing them lowers AUC-PR from 0.831 to 0.575 for XGBoost and from 0.823 to 0.484 for GraphSAGE. Removing the distance to the river costs 0.019 for XGBoost and 0.070 for GraphSAGE. Removing the rainfall or network features leaves XGBoost unchanged, as expected for the constant rainfall features. For GraphSAGE, the same constant rainfall features change AUC-PR by $-0.019$, which shows that its run-to-run variation is about 0.02. The graph ablations ($-0.016$ without edges, $+0.001$ with one layer) fall within this variation, so the benchmark gives no evidence that road connectivity improves prediction; only a third layer clearly hurts ($-0.070$).

\subsection{Downstream Routing}
Table~\ref{tab:routing} compares the routing results of the six models with the two baselines, and Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff} plots flood exposure against travel-time increase. At $\lambda=5$, the MLP gave the fewest flooded segments (10.35 per trip) and the shortest flooded length (625.8~m), 41.2\% less than the shortest-time route, at a median travel-time increase of 16.5\%. XGBoost followed (639.8~m, +13.5\%), and random forest, the best classifier, ranked third (683.6~m) with the smallest detour among the nonlinear models (+7.6\%). The routing ranking therefore differs from the AUC-PR ranking at the top, although LR is last on both. Part of the difference comes from detour size: the models' probabilities lead to different detours at the same $\lambda$. Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto} compares the models at equal travel-time cost as $\lambda$ varies; there the curves of the nonlinear models lie close together, with XGBoost lowest over most of the range. Even the oracle route still crossed at least one flooded segment in 94.7\% of the OD pairs (8.03 segments and 485.6~m per trip), because 42.5\% of the network is impassable and long detours are penalized.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} and Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} show how XGBoost's results change with the block size and the labeling. AUC-PR varied between 0.831 and 0.883 across block sizes of 250 to 1000~m, with the highest value for the smallest blocks, which leave more neighbors of each test segment in the training set. The alternative labelings give AUC-PR values near 0.99, but these are close to their much higher no-skill baselines: excluding flooded-but-passable segments leaves 86.3\% of the remaining segments flooded, and labeling every segment deeper than 10~cm as flooded makes 93.2\% flooded. Their AUC-ROC values (0.940 and 0.853) show that the task is easier when the 10--30~cm segments are removed and harder when they count as flooded.
