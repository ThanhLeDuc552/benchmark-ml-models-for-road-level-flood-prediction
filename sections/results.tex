\subsection{Classification Benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:models} compares the six models under spatial cross-validation. Random forest achieved the highest AUC-PR ($0.840 \pm 0.077$), followed by the MLP ($0.833 \pm 0.067$), XGBoost ($0.831 \pm 0.081$), and GraphSAGE ($0.823 \pm 0.083$); logistic regression was lowest ($0.784 \pm 0.111$). These differences are small compared with the variation between folds. The Friedman test found no significant difference among the six models ($\chi^2 = 7.86$, $p = 0.164$), and pairwise Wilcoxon tests against random forest gave $p = 0.0625$ for LR, the smallest value possible with five folds, and $p \ge 0.31$ for the other models. GraphSAGE reached the highest recall on impassable segments ($0.852 \pm 0.043$) and F1 ($0.791 \pm 0.057$), and random forest the lowest Brier score ($0.127 \pm 0.012$).

Every model scored higher under random than under spatial cross-validation (Table~\ref{tab:models}), with optimism gaps $\Delta_m$ from $+0.034$ (LR) to $+0.094$ (random forest). The tree ensembles were inflated most (random forest $+0.094$, XGBoost $+0.085$), followed by GraphSAGE ($+0.077$), the MLP ($+0.054$), and the SVM ($+0.047$). Random forest would appear near-perfect under random cross-validation (AUC-ROC 0.992), whereas under spatial cross-validation all models except LR lie between 0.889 and 0.897.

\subsection{Risk Map and Feature Effects}
Of the 2,059 segments, 876 (42.5\%) are impassable in the simulation ($y=1$), and they cluster along the low-lying bank of the Han River and around natural depressions, including the Dien Bien Phu underpass. Fig.~\ref{fig:riskmap} maps the out-of-fold flood probability of each segment for random forest, the best classifier. \NRx{verify the share of segments with $p \ge 0.66$ (currently stated as 36.4\%) from the notebook} of the segments fall in the high-risk class. The TreeSHAP values of XGBoost rank the top features as depression depth (mean $|\text{SHAP}| = 1.592$), TPI (0.992), distance to the Han River (0.499), and mean elevation (0.486). By each model's own importance ranking (Section~\ref{sec:cv}), terrain features rank highest for all models: depression depth is first for four models and second for the other two, and TPI is first for LR and random forest. Network features such as betweenness and segment length rank low for every model, including GraphSAGE.

\subsection{Feature-Group Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports AUC-PR when each feature group is removed. Terrain features matter most: removing them lowers AUC-PR from 0.831 to 0.575 for XGBoost and from 0.823 to 0.484 for GraphSAGE. Removing the rainfall or network features leaves XGBoost unchanged, as expected for the constant rainfall features. For GraphSAGE, the same constant rainfall features change AUC-PR by $-0.019$, which shows that its run-to-run variation is about 0.02. The graph ablations ($-0.016$ without edges, $+0.001$ with one layer) fall within this variation, so the benchmark gives no evidence that road connectivity improves prediction; only a third layer clearly hurts ($-0.070$).

\subsection{Downstream Routing}
Table~\ref{tab:routing} compares the routing results of the six models with the two baselines. At $\lambda=5$, the MLP gave the fewest flooded segments (10.35 per trip) and the shortest flooded length (625.8~m), 41.2\% less than the shortest-time route, at a median travel-time increase of 16.5\%. XGBoost followed (639.8~m, +13.5\%), and random forest, the best classifier, ranked third (683.6~m) with the smallest detour among the nonlinear models (+7.6\%). The routing ranking therefore differs from the AUC-PR ranking at the top, although LR is last on both. Part of the difference comes from detour size, because the models' probabilities lead to different detours at the same $\lambda$; at equal travel-time cost (Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}), the curves of the nonlinear models lie close together, with XGBoost lowest over most of the range. Even the oracle route still crossed at least one flooded segment in 94.7\% of the OD pairs (8.03 segments and 485.6~m per trip), because 42.5\% of the network is impassable and long detours are penalized.

\subsection{Efficiency and Sensitivity}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
All models are small and fast. LR has 19 parameters, the MLP 3,329, and GraphSAGE 3,297, while the approximate sizes of XGBoost, the SVM, and RF are 3,200, 37,000, and 50,000. All models trained in under 0.5~s and predicted the whole district in under 90~ms. LR was the fastest to train (0.01~s) and predict (2.4~ms), the SVM the slowest to predict (88.8~ms), and routing took 2.3~ms per OD pair. Sensitivity tests with XGBoost vary the block size and the labeling. AUC-PR was 0.883, 0.831, and 0.847 for block sizes of 250, 500, and 1000~m, highest for the smallest blocks, which leave more neighbors of each test segment in the training set. The alternative labelings give AUC-PR values near 0.99 (0.991 and 0.987), but these are close to their much higher no-skill baselines: excluding flooded-but-passable segments leaves 86.3\% of the remaining segments flooded, and labeling every segment deeper than 10~cm as flooded makes 93.2\% flooded. Their AUC-ROC values (0.940 and 0.853, against 0.893 for the main labeling) show that the task is easier when the 10--30~cm segments are removed and harder when they count as flooded.
